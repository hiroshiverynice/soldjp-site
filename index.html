Why searching in English isn't enough

Brand names translate. The words that matter don't.

Yahoo! Auctions quietly normalises well-known brands, so English gets you surprisingly far — right up until it doesn't. Type a category word instead of a brand and you silently lose 90% of the market. You never see an error. You just see a thinner, more expensive market than the one that actually exists.

The unit of a decision is a model, not a keyword

“Nikon lens” is not a product. It's 45,344 different ones.

Most sourcing advice dies here. A category-level average is arithmetic performed on unrelated objects. SoldJP measures the spread between the 10th and 90th percentile and refuses to give you a confident number when the spread says your search is still a bucket.

What it does

A floor, not an average

Percentiles from every closed sale in the last 120 days, split by condition grade, with a maximum bid that already accounts for eBay's 13.25% final value fee, payment fees, freight and proxy commission.

Velocity and contest

Sales per day and the share of lots that drew more than one bidder. A high median with no competing bids is a trap; you'll see it before you're holding the inventory.

Both languages, one search

Your English query is expanded into the Japanese terms sellers actually write, including the katakana and kanji forms that never match a romanised search.
